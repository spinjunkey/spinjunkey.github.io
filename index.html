INTEL DIGITAL HEALTH · HUMAN-CENTERED PRODUCT INNOVATION

Redesigning the process, not just the product.

The interface wasn't the real problem. Human understanding was entering the development process too late to shape the product.

THE MESS

A previous digital-health project exposed a structural flaw in the development process. Engineering built. Design reacted. Changes became expensive. Teams thrashed. UX had entered too late to influence major product decisions.

THE REFRAME

Instead of asking, “How do we design a better interface?” we asked: How might we redesign the product-development process so human needs shape the product before engineering decisions become difficult to change?

INVESTIGATE

For Arlington, an independent-living platform, the team moved upstream. Extensive ethnographic fieldwork and industry research helped us understand customers, end users and the broader system of people and workflows surrounding the product.

CREATE + EXPERIMENT

We introduced upfront design, workflow analysis, requirements, ideation, low-fidelity prototyping and formative user testing before major development decisions were locked. Downstream design then translated that learning into user flows, wireframes, interaction models and production interfaces.

WHAT CHANGED

UX gained greater ownership from product definition through implementation. The approach reduced avoidable rework and kept human needs visible as the product moved into development. At the time of the original case study, Arlington had been deployed at two major Aging Services Providers with 140 units in the field.

THE BIGGER LEARNING

Human-centered innovation isn't just a design methodology. The organization has to create the conditions that allow it to work. We weren't merely redesigning the thing. We were redesigning how the organization thought about designing the thing.

THE PATTERN

Investigate → Reframe → Create → Experiment → Connect → Translate → Act

Methods: ethnographic research, systems thinking, workflow analysis, prototyping, formative testing, interaction design and cross-functional collaboration.

INTEL EMPLOYEE EXPERIENCE · HUMAN-CENTERED ORGANIZATIONAL INNOVATION

Listening at the speed of experience.

Traditional employee listening could tell the organization what had happened. The opportunity was to understand what was happening while there was still time to respond.

THE MESS

Intel already had annual surveys, pulse checks and manager feedback. The problem wasn't a lack of information. It was time. By the time concerns surfaced through periodic channels, small issues could already have grown into larger cultural, engagement or productivity problems.

THE REFRAME

The obvious response was to improve surveys or run them more often. Instead, we explored a different possibility: move from periodically measuring employee sentiment to continuously sensing employee experience.

DESIGNING THE MODEL

I developed a strategy connecting rapid sentiment indicators, employee touchpoints, privacy, transparency and trust with a clear path from incoming signals to action. Faster data alone wasn't enough. The organization also needed a way to interpret and respond to what it heard.

EXPERIMENT

Rather than attempting an enterprise rollout, we piloted the model with targeted groups. The pilot tested ways to capture sentiment quickly, identify emerging trends and route insights toward people who could respond.

WHAT CHANGED

The pilot enabled earlier identification of emerging patterns than formal surveys and shortened the response cycle from months to days or weeks. More importantly, it demonstrated a different relationship with employee feedback: listen, identify, route, respond and learn.

THE BIGGER LEARNING

Speed alone isn't innovation. Real-time listening becomes useful only when the human system around it—trust, interpretation and response—is designed as carefully as the sensing mechanism.

THE PATTERN

Investigate → Reframe → Create → Experiment → Learn → Act

Methods: experience strategy, employee listening, systems thinking, technology exploration, framework development and pilot experimentation.

FORMFACTOR · HUMAN-CENTERED EXPERIENCE INNOVATION

Designing the employee experience before the workplace existed.

A new manufacturing facility created an opportunity to do more than launch programs. It created a chance to intentionally shape how people would experience the culture from the moment they joined.

THE MESS

A large population of new hires would be forming their understanding of FormFactor at the same time the new site itself was taking shape. The organization needed to support operational reality while carrying forward the cultural strengths it valued.

THE QUESTION

How might we intentionally design an employee experience that helps people understand the culture, feel connected to it and experience it consistently from the moment they join?

INVESTIGATE

Discovery included stakeholder interviews and workshops, review of existing onboarding and engagement practices, persona development and exploration of cultural strengths worth preserving. The goal was to understand the intersection of employee needs, culture and operational reality.

THE REFRAME

The project could have been framed as, “What employee programs should we put in the new facility?” A more useful frame was: What should it feel like to become part of FormFactor here?

DESIGN + TRANSLATE

I helped define an ideal future-state experience across onboarding, belonging, moments that matter, employer brand, celebration, connection and recognition—then translated that vision into functional and experiential requirements for HR, Communications, Operations and Facilities.

WHAT CHANGED

The work established an actionable roadmap and a shared framework for a connected, culturally aligned employee experience. It created greater clarity across touchpoints and cross-functional ownership around how different parts of the organization contributed to the whole.

THE BIGGER LEARNING

A new workplace is not just a physical environment. Every process, message, ritual, space and interaction teaches a new employee something about what the organization values. The work began with programs. The more useful problem was experience.

THE PATTERN

Investigate → Reframe → Create → Convene → Translate → Act

Methods: discovery, stakeholder interviews, workshops, personas, experience design, future-state visioning, facilitation and requirements development.
